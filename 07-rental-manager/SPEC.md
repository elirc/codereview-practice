# 07 — Rental Property Manager

## 1. Purpose

A back-office tool for a small landlord or property manager: properties, units, tenants, leases, and a rent ledger per lease with recurring rent charges, proration, payments and late fees. Tenants do not log in. Read `../CONVENTIONS.md` first.

## 4. Domain model

- **users**: `id`, `email`, `password_hash`, `name`, `role`, `active`.
- **properties**: `id`, `name`, `address`. **units**: `id`, `property_id`, `label` (unique per property), `bedrooms`, `market_rent_cents`.
- **tenants**: `id`, `full_name`, `email?`, `phone?`.
- **leases**: `id`, `unit_id`, `status` (`draft` | `active` | `ended`), `start_date`, `end_date?` (inclusive last day of occupancy; null = month-to-month), `rent_cents` (> 0), `due_day` (1–31), `grace_days` (0–15), `late_fee_kind` (`none` | `flat` | `percent`), `late_fee_flat_cents`, `late_fee_bps` (basis points, 1–2500), `late_fee_cap_cents?`, `deposit_cents`, `activated_at?`, `ended_at?`. Check `end_date IS NULL OR end_date >= start_date`.
  - A unit cannot have two leases in (`active`) whose date ranges overlap: exclusion constraint on `unit_id` with `daterange(start_date, COALESCE(end_date + 1, 'infinity'), '[)')` where `status = 'active'` (requires `btree_gist`).
- **lease_tenants**: `(lease_id, tenant_id)`, `is_primary`. At least one tenant, exactly one primary, to activate.
- **ledger_entries**: `id` (bigserial — ordering matters), `lease_id`, `entry_date` (civil date the entry takes effect), `kind` (`rent` | `late_fee` | `other_charge` | `deposit_charge` | `payment` | `credit` | `reversal`), `amount_cents` (signed: charges positive; payments and credits negative; a reversal has the opposite sign of its target; never 0), `period` (`YYYY-MM` text, set for `rent` and `late_fee`), `reverses_entry_id?`, `idempotency_key` (unique, not null), `memo`, `created_by?` (null = system), `created_at`.
  - Check constraints tie sign to kind. Partial unique index on `reverses_entry_id` (an entry is reversed at most once).
  - Trigger rejects `UPDATE` and `DELETE` on the table.
- **job_runs**: `id`, `job`, `as_of`, `started_at`, `finished_at?`, `status` (`running` | `ok` | `partial` | `failed`), `leases_processed`, `entries_posted`, `errors` (jsonb array of `{lease_id, error}`).

There is no stored balance column anywhere. Balance of a lease as of date D = `SUM(amount_cents) WHERE entry_date <= D`. Positive = tenant owes.

## 5. Domain rules

### 5.1 Ledger

- Append-only. Corrections are made by posting a `reversal` (amount = −target amount, `entry_date` = today, `reverses_entry_id` = target) and, if needed, a new correct entry.
- A `reversal` cannot itself be reversed. An already reversed entry cannot be reversed again (`409 already_reversed`; the unique index is the backstop and its violation maps to the same error).
- Every post carries an idempotency key. Posting with an existing key is a no-op that returns the existing entry (`INSERT … ON CONFLICT (idempotency_key) DO NOTHING` followed by a select). Keys: `rent:<lease>:<YYYY-MM>`, `late:<lease>:<YYYY-MM>`, `deposit:<lease>`, `reversal:<entry>`, and for manual entries a UUID generated when the form is rendered (hidden field), so a double submit posts once.
- Manual entries: `payment` and `credit` take a positive amount from the user and are stored negative; `other_charge` positive. `entry_date` may be backdated up to 60 days and not later than today. Entries can be posted only to `active` or `ended` leases, with `entry_date >= lease.start_date`.
- Posting to a lease takes `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the lease row first, so that postings and job runs for one lease are serialised.
- Running balance display: entries ordered by `(entry_date, id)`, cumulative sum computed in SQL with a window function; the handler does not accumulate in Go.

### 5.2 Calendar

- `calendar.ClampDay(year, month, day)` = `min(day, daysInMonth(year, month))`.
- Due date for period `YYYY-MM` = `ClampDay(Y, M, lease.due_day)`. Always computed from the lease's original `due_day`, never from the previous month's due date (Jan 31 → Feb 28/29 → Mar 31).
- Adding days is done on civil dates (no `time.Duration` arithmetic across DST).
- Leap years follow the Gregorian rule.

### 5.3 Rent schedule and proration

A lease's rent periods are calendar months from the month of `start_date` to the month of `end_date` (or open-ended).

For period P = month M of year Y, with `dim` = days in that month:
- `occ_start = max(start_date, first of M)`, `occ_end = min(end_date, last of M)` (or last of M when no end date). `days = occ_end − occ_start + 1` (both inclusive).
- If `days == dim`: charge = `rent_cents`.
- Otherwise prorated: `charge = round_half_up(rent_cents × days / dim)`, computed in integers as `(rent_cents*days*2 + dim) / (2*dim)`.
- **Charge date** (the `entry_date`, and the date the charge is due):
  - First period: `max(start_date, due date of P)`. A first period is never dated before the lease starts; if the due day has already passed in the start month, it is dated on `start_date`.
  - All other periods: due date of P.
- If rounding yields a charge of 0 cents, the period is skipped (no entry).

Rent changes: not supported in place. To change rent, end the lease and create a new one.

### 5.4 Posting rent (job `post-rent`)

For as-of date D (default: today), for each `active` or `ended` lease, in its own transaction with the lease row locked. Ended leases are included because a lease can be ended before the charge date of its final month, or before the job has run for that month; its schedule stops at the month of `end_date`, so nothing is posted beyond it.
- For every period P from the lease's first period up to and including the month of D: if the charge date of P is `<= D` and no ledger entry exists with key `rent:<lease>:<P>`, post it with `entry_date` = charge date (not D), memo stating the period and, when prorated, the day count.
- This catches up any number of missed days or months in one run, and running it twice for the same D posts nothing new.
- A reversed rent charge is **not** re-posted (the key exists). To re-bill, the manager posts an `other_charge`.

### 5.5 Allocation and late fees

Allocation is computed, not stored. Given a lease's entries with `entry_date <= cutoff`:
- Charges (`rent`, `late_fee`, `other_charge`, `deposit_charge`) net of their reversals form the list of open items, ordered by `(entry_date, id)`. A reversed charge and its reversal are both removed from consideration. A reversed payment/credit and its reversal are likewise removed.
- Total credits = sum of absolute values of remaining `payment` and `credit` entries. Credits are applied to open items oldest first until exhausted. The result gives each charge an unpaid remainder.

Late fee for period P (job `assess-late-fees`, as-of D, per lease in its own transaction with the lease row locked):
- Skip if `late_fee_kind = 'none'`, if key `late:<lease>:<P>` exists, or if P has no un-reversed rent entry.
- `deadline = charge date of P + grace_days` (civil days; no weekend or holiday adjustment). The fee is assessable when `D > deadline`.
- Compute allocation with `cutoff = deadline` (payments dated on the deadline count as on time; payments dated after it do not). Let `unpaid` = unpaid remainder of P's rent entry. If `unpaid <= 0`, no fee is posted and nothing is recorded; the period is evaluated again on later runs, so reversing an on-time payment afterwards makes the period assessable.
- Fee: `flat` → `late_fee_flat_cents`; `percent` → `round_half_up(unpaid × late_fee_bps / 10000)` = `(unpaid*bps*2 + 10000) / 20000`; then `min(fee, late_fee_cap_cents)` when a cap is set. A computed fee of 0 is not posted.
- Posted with `entry_date = deadline + 1 day`, `period = P`, regardless of when the job actually runs.
- Late fees are never charged on late fees or other charges: only the rent entry of P is the basis. (Late fees do join the open-item list for allocation of later payments.)
- The job never removes a fee. If a backdated payment later shows the rent was paid on time, a manager reverses the fee manually.
- For `ended` leases, fees are still assessed for periods up to the end month.

### 5.6 Lease lifecycle

- `draft` → `active`: requires tenants (one primary), unit free for the range (exclusion constraint → `409 unit_occupied`). On activation, if `deposit_cents > 0`, post `deposit_charge` dated `start_date`. Then run the rent posting logic for this lease as of today (so a lease activated with a past start date is caught up immediately).
- `active` → `ended`: manager supplies `end_date` (≥ `start_date`; it may shorten an existing end date or close an open-ended lease, and may be in the past). Rent already posted for periods after the new end date, or for a full final month that is now partial, is **not** auto-adjusted; the end-lease screen shows a computed suggested credit = (posted rent for affected periods) − (rent due under the new end date), and the manager may post it as a `credit` in the same transaction by ticking a box. The suggestion is computed by the same proration function.
- Draft leases can be edited freely or deleted. Active leases: only `grace_days`, late-fee settings and tenants can be edited; changes affect only fees assessed afterwards.

### 5.7 Reports

- **Lease statement**: entries with running balance for a date range, opening balance = balance as of the day before the range.
- **Rent roll** as of D: per active lease — unit, primary tenant, rent, balance as of D, oldest unpaid charge date, days past due.
- **Aged receivables** as of D: per lease, unpaid remainders from allocation (cutoff D) bucketed by age of the charge's `entry_date`: 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days (`age = D − entry_date` in days; 30 falls in the first bucket, 31 in the second). Leases with a credit balance show the credit separately and zeros in buckets. Bucket totals plus credits reconcile to the sum of balances.

## 7. Background jobs

`rentmgr worker` runs a ticker (every 15 minutes). On each tick, for each job in order `post-rent` then `assess-late-fees`: if no `job_runs` row with status `ok` exists for (job, today), run it with as-of = today.

- Single runner: a job run takes `pg_try_advisory_lock(<job-specific key>)` on a dedicated connection for its duration; if not acquired, the run is skipped. The lock is released in a `defer` and the connection returned.
- A `job_runs` row is inserted at start (`running`) and finalised at the end. A stale `running` row from a crashed process does not block later runs (the advisory lock is the mutual exclusion; the row is informational) and is marked `failed` by the next run of that job.
- Each lease is processed in its own transaction. An error on one lease is recorded in `errors`, logged with the lease id, and the run continues; final status `partial` if any lease failed, which means the next tick will run the job again (already-posted entries are skipped by key).
- Context cancellation (SIGINT/SIGTERM) stops between leases; the current transaction finishes or rolls back; status `partial`.
- Manual runs (`run-job`, admin button) accept an explicit `as_of` no later than today and follow the same path.
- Because entries are dated by rule rather than by run time, and keys are deterministic, a job run on any later day produces the same ledger as timely runs.

## 8. Edge cases

1. `due_day = 31`: due dates are Jan 31, Feb 28 (29 in leap years), Mar 31, Apr 30.
2. `due_day = 29`, February of a non-leap year → Feb 28; March → Mar 29.
3. Lease starts Jan 31 with `due_day = 1`: first period prorated 1/31, dated Jan 31; February charged in full, dated Feb 1.
4. Lease starts Feb 15 in a leap year: 15 days of 29.
5. Lease starts on the 1st with `due_day = 5`: full first month, dated the 5th.
6. Lease starts on the 10th with `due_day = 5`: prorated first month dated the 10th; with `due_day = 20`: dated the 20th.
7. Lease starts and ends in the same month (e.g. Mar 10–Mar 20): one prorated period of 11 days.
8. Lease ending on the last day of a month: final month is a full charge, no proration.
9. Proration rounding: rent 100,000 cents, 10 days of 31 → 32,258; 1 day of 31 on rent 100,001 → 3,226.
10. Running `post-rent` twice for the same day posts nothing the second time; running it after three missed months posts three charges with their own due dates.
11. Two workers started at once: one skips; no duplicate entries.
12. Double-submitted payment form posts one entry.
13. Payment dated on the grace deadline → no late fee; dated the next day → fee.
14. Grace 0: fee assessable the day after the due date.
15. Partial payment before the deadline: percent fee is on the unpaid remainder; flat fee is the full flat amount.
16. Overpayment in month 1 carries forward by allocation and covers part of month 2's rent; month 2's fee basis reflects it.
17. An unpaid old `other_charge` absorbs a payment before the current month's rent (oldest first), which can leave rent unpaid and incur a fee.
18. Late fee from month 1 unpaid does not itself generate a fee in month 2, and is not part of month 2's basis.
19. Percent fee with cap: fee is the smaller of computed and cap; a computed fee that rounds to 0 is not posted.
20. Late-fee job run 10 days after the deadline posts the fee dated deadline + 1.
21. Backdated payment (entry_date before the deadline) recorded after the fee was assessed: fee remains; manager can reverse it; job does not re-assess (key exists).
22. Reversing a payment restores the balance; allocation ignores both the payment and its reversal for all cutoffs.
23. Reversing an entry twice (double click or two users) → one reversal.
24. Attempt to reverse a reversal → refused.
25. `UPDATE`/`DELETE` on `ledger_entries` fails at the database.
26. Statement opening balance for a range starting mid-history equals the sum of all earlier entries; running balances are continuous across ranges.
27. Entries on the same `entry_date` are ordered by id in running balances and in allocation.
28. Activating a lease whose unit already has an overlapping active lease → `unit_occupied`; a lease starting the day after another's `end_date` is accepted.
29. Activating a lease with `start_date` three months ago posts deposit and three (or four) rent charges immediately, correctly dated.
30. Ending a lease mid-month after that month's full rent was posted: suggested credit equals full rent minus prorated amount; nothing is posted unless the manager confirms.
31. No rent is posted for periods after `end_date`'s month; an `ended` lease with unpaid rent still gets late fees for periods through its end month.
32. Aging boundaries: a charge exactly 30 days old is in 0–30; 31 days in 31–60; totals reconcile with balances including credit-balance leases.
33. "Today" near midnight UTC: with `APP_TIMEZONE=America/Chicago`, at 03:00 UTC on the 1st it is still the last day of the previous month, so the new month's rent (due on the 1st) is not yet posted.
34. Amount input `12.345`, `-5`, `0`, `1e3`, `1,000.00`, or empty → validation error; `1000` and `1000.5` parse to 100000 and 100050 cents.
35. One lease failing during a job (e.g. constraint error) does not prevent other leases from being processed; run status is `partial` with the error recorded.
36. Manager reversing an entry older than 60 days → forbidden; admin allowed.
37. A lease ended on the 10th, before that month's rent was posted (due day 15): the next `post-rent` run posts the prorated final month (10 days), dated the 15th, even though the lease is already `ended`; no later period is ever posted for it.

## 9. Non-goals

Tenant portal, online payments, bank reconciliation, rent increases within a lease, utilities/meter billing, deposits refund workflow (beyond manual credit), multi-currency, document storage, email/SMS, owner statements, accounting export.

## 10. Acceptance criteria

- The ledger is strictly append-only and every balance, statement, allocation and report is derived from it.
- Rent charges and late fees are posted exactly once per period with dates determined by the rules, independent of when jobs run.
- Proration and month-end clamping match 5.2–5.3 exactly.
- Jobs are safe to re-run, safe with concurrent workers, and isolate per-lease failures.
- All errors are handled explicitly; no ignored errors.
- All edge cases in section 8 hold.

## 11. Required tests

Unit (table-driven, no DB):
- `calendar`: days in month, leap years, clamp, add days across month/year ends, date in zone near midnight.
- `money`: parse and format decimal strings; rounding helpers.
- `billing` schedule: periods, charge dates and amounts for the start/end shapes in edge cases 1–9.
- `billing` allocation: FIFO, overpayment carry, reversals excluded, same-day ordering, cutoff.
- `billing` late-fee decision: grace boundary, flat, percent on remainder, cap, zero fee, basis excludes fees and other charges.
- Aging buckets and reconciliation.

Integration (Postgres):
- Ledger posting idempotency by key (sequential and concurrent goroutines).
- Reversal rules including concurrent double reversal; append-only trigger.
- `post-rent`: catch-up, re-run no-op, activation catch-up, no posting past end month, final prorated month posted for a lease that was ended before its charge date.
- `assess-late-fees`: each decision branch end-to-end; late run dates the fee correctly; no re-assessment.
- Advisory-lock single runner (two concurrent runs).
- Per-lease failure isolation and `partial` status.
- Lease activation overlap constraint; adjacent leases allowed.
- Statement running balance and opening balance via the SQL window query.
- HTTP: role matrix for write routes (viewer/manager/admin), CSRF required, manual entry validation, double-submit, JSON statement endpoint envelope.
